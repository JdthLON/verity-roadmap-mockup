At a glance

Verity has achieved what few charities do, almost entirely through volunteers: it was the only patient organisation on the global committee that renamed the condition, it set up the UK's first parliamentary group on it, and it has a seat on the committee for the NICE guideline due in January.

Your pack asks the Interim CEO for an agreed roadmap within six weeks, and for two jobs above all: an income base that can fund a permanent CEO, and becoming an employer for the first time. This is how I would start. It is a scaffold, built only from the pack and public information. It shows how I would structure the work, who is involved in each step, what waits on whom, and what six months can and cannot deliver. Much of it would change once I knew Verity from the inside.

I would love to build it out with you. Whoever is appointed, you are very welcome to use it.

Judith Mueller, candidate for the Interim CEO role

Before the start date: goodwill days

As goodwill on my side, not charged, and at times that suit you: getting to know everyone before the contract begins, so that day one starts with what is needed.

  1. 1Contract signedA contract for services, including confidentiality, signed before the introductions, for example in the last week of November. The contract clock starts on Monday 4 January.
  2. 2IntroductionsOne-hour calls at times that suit you, with each trustee (including the trustees who joined in 2026) and the administrator.
  3. 3Notes backWritten notes from each call within two days, for each person to add to.
  4. 4Pre-start questionsAbout you, a two-week log of your Verity time, a list of Verity's systems, and key documents.
  5. 5Day one, 4 JanuaryPreparation, then a short debrief with everyone: anything else I need for my first two weeks.

If the appointment comes later, the same steps simply move, and funding deadlines that have passed drop out.

Pre-start questions for trustees and the administrator

  • About you: Your skills, what you do for Verity today, what you would like to do in the longer term and what would be sustainable for you, the time you have for the transition, and your networks.
  • A two-week log of your Verity time: Only the time you spend on Verity, not your whole day: what you do, when, for how long, and which skills it draws on. It is the baseline for test 3: trustees spending materially less time on operational delivery.
  • Systems list: What Verity uses today, what you would like, what is missing, and who holds each account.

Documents and information, once the contract is signed

  • The grant strategy
  • Current funders, their grant end dates and terms
  • The latest figures and management accounts
  • Existing policies, insurance and registrations
  • The 2027 Board dates
  • How the APPG will be funded from April 2027
  • The media set-up: budget, processes, who prepares
  • Key relationships, and who holds them
  • The NICE publication on 27 January: your plans and any embargo arrangements

First conversations in January

  • How the interim role is funded
  • The pace of the media and stakeholder handover, and which relationships trustees keep
  • The Board's position on industry funding
  • The days, salary and timing the Board has in mind for the permanent post

What six months can deliver, and what it cannot

Six months at two days a week is enough to build the foundations properly. Securing the funding for a permanent CEO, and appointing one, depends on funders' decision times and a recruitment process, and realistically concludes around month twelve.

Six months at two days a week can deliver properly

  • The roadmap agreed by the Board, with people, systems and money mapped
  • The Board report, scheme of delegation and risk register in use
  • The income strategy agreed, the pipeline live and the first applications in
  • The employer-readiness plan signed off, providers chosen and policies drafted
  • The permanent CEO role defined and costed, with a recruitment plan
  • A handover pack

What it cannot, because of lead times rather than effort

  • The funding for a permanent CEO. Trust decisions take 16 weeks to 6 months, so applications sent in months 2 to 4 are decided in months 6 to 10.
  • A permanent CEO in post. A CEO search typically takes around six months, plus one to three months' notice, and it needs the funding to be reasonably clear first.
  • Employer systems fully in use. They are only truly in use once someone is employed.
  • Trustee-dependent work at full speed. Board reports need trustees' input, sign-offs wait for Board meetings, and relationship records need every trustee.

The roadmap therefore shows months 7 to 12 at one day a week, for modelling purposes only: it could be anything from none to two days a week, to be discussed and agreed. The table shows what each option would mean in total.

Without them, the first full roadmap would come around week 4 rather than week 2, leaving less time to test it with trustees before week 6. The pack offers a six-month contract "with view to extend up to 12 months" (page 12). Totals count two days every week after the ramp-up.

Beyond the interim: what a permanent team would need (2028 to 2030)

For the Board to shape. A three-year horizon fits multi-year core grants (one to three years), the renaming's managed three-year transition, and a first strategy cycle for a permanent CEO. The longer direction is the mission itself, reviewed every year.

Income

Several income streams so that no single funder dominates; multi-year core grants; regular giving; earned income including PMOS TIME; a reserves policy; the APPG funded separately.

People

The permanent CEO in post; a staffing map shaped by the people-and-systems decision; clear volunteer roles; media work that does not rest on one person.

Governance

Scheme of delegation, Board report and risk register embedded; a Board skills and renewal plan; an annual review.

Systems

Supporter database; finance, HR and media processes; the dashboard with login and comments; plus additional systems, to be mapped.

Mission

NICE implementation; the renaming's managed three-year transition, with the new name integrated into the 2028 international guideline update; the APPG's recommendations; the research priorities; reach into underdiagnosed communities.

What this dashboard does now, and what I would add

A snapshot built from public information. It shows what Verity and its Interim CEO need to deliver, and by when: who does what, how many days each step takes, and how long it waits on others, with every figure marked as real, an estimate or still a gap. Each month the same page would show progress against the plan agreed in week 6.

  • Secure login: Password-protected access.
  • Comments in one place: Trustees, the team and invited partners can comment on any item, so all input sits together.
  • One source of truth: Updated each month from one spreadsheet, so the Board pack is produced, not written.
  • Views for different people: The Board sees everything, the team their own actions, invited partners only what they are shown.
  • Owners update their own work: Each owner updates the status of their own steps.
  • A history: Each month's version is kept, so progress and decisions can be traced.
  • Reminders and export: Deadline reminders from the calendar, and a PDF for the Board papers.
  • Safe by design: No personal or health data on the dashboard, and access logged.
